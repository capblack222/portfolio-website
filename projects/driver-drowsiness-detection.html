Driver Drowsiness Detection and Alert System

published · 2023

Real-time driver-fatigue detection on a Raspberry Pi, using a camera and no specialised in-vehicle hardware.

  • Hinweis ASIT 2023

    published and presented

  • Raspberry Pi

    commodity hardware, in-vehicle

  • Real time

    eye-state analysis per frame

the problem

Fatigue detection systems that ship in cars rely on instrumentation most vehicles do not have — steering-angle sensors, lane-departure cameras, driver-facing infrared. The population that would benefit most from a drowsiness alert is the one driving older vehicles without any of it.

The constraint was therefore the hardware, not the algorithm: whatever the detection did, it had to run in real time on a Raspberry Pi with an ordinary camera, inside a moving vehicle.

approach

  • Built detection around eye-state analysis. Facial landmark tracking with OpenCV and Dlib, with sustained eye closure over consecutive frames as the fatigue signal rather than any single frame.
  • Targeted commodity hardware from the start. Designing for the Pi constrained every later choice, which is the opposite of building something accurate and discovering afterwards that it needs a GPU.
  • Published the findings. Presented at Hinweis ASIT 2023, the Second International Conference on Advances in Software Engineering and Information Technology.

what I would change

Landmark tracking degrades in exactly the conditions where drowsiness matters most: night driving, low light, and glasses. A system that works well in daylight and poorly at 3am is solving the easier half of the problem.

I would also want a false-positive measurement rather than a detection-rate one. An alert system that cries wolf gets switched off, and then its detection rate is zero.
